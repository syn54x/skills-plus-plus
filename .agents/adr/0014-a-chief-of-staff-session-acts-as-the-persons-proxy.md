# A Chief of Staff session acts as the person's Proxy, behind one gate, and keeps a Wiki

spp's paths each start when a person types a command, so a person running several repos spends their attention starting, chasing and relaying work that needs no judgement from them. A **Chief of Staff session** takes that over. A person starts one anywhere, including a folder above several checkouts, gives it goals at the start or along the way, and it acts as their **Proxy** for each of them: it sends work out, follows it through, and brings the **Principal** only **Escalations**, **Challenges** and a veto list of what it settled in their name. It is a session role beside the **Build session** and adds no sixth path; the **Five paths** are some of its tools. One gate bounds it: merging to a protected branch, releasing and closing an **Epic** each need the person's explicit word, every time. Everything else it does on its own and reports. Approving each step lost because a Chief of Staff that asks before every action saves the person nothing. It keeps what it learns in a **Wiki**, a dated record that outlives the session, sits above the repos and tracks every goal, decisions included, on one condition: whatever a repo already records (its glossary, ADRs, issues, code) is cited at a commit and never copied. A Wiki narrowed to preferences and recurring problems lost because decisions that span repos have no repo to live in. The harness's own memory lost because only one harness has it and it keeps no history. The skill is adapted from Matt Pocock's `chief-of-staff`. It starts only model-invoked skills (see [ADR 0004](./0004-sdd-pipeline-invocation-graph.md)), so for a step whose entry is user-invoked it asks the person to type it; splitting such an entry is decided case by case, as the person finds they want the Chief of Staff to do more.
